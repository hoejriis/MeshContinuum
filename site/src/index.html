A radio network that works without the Internet. A connected experience when you have it.

Small radios that pass messages to each other, with no mobile network. MECON makes them more useful when they are connected.

MeshCore radios carry text messages for kilometres, from radio to radio, with no phone mast or server. MECON is an optional layer on top: it combines what many participating radios hear, keeps the history, and helps look after the radios. Take MECON away and the radios keep talking, exactly as before.

Where things stand: a private beta on mecon.cloud, by invitation. The radio firmware is public as source and test releases; the server software is not published yet. Some features described here are planned. Availability

What is MeshCore?

MeshCore is open-source software for small, low-cost LoRa radios. Each radio passes messages on to the next, so a message can cross a town or a valley with no mobile network, no Internet and no server. It keeps working in a power cut, in the mountains or at sea. Anyone can join with a radio and a phone app. A few words you will meet:

Companion
Your personal radio, paired with an app on your phone or computer.
Repeater
A radio left on in a good spot, often high up, passing others' messages on to extend the mesh.
Room server
A small bulletin board on the mesh that keeps posts for you.
Observer
A radio that also reports what it hears to an online service. MECON learns from observers as well as your own radios.

MeshCore in brief explains the rest, and what you need to begin.

What MECON adds

Many receivers, one picture

A radio only hears what reaches it while it is on. MECON combines what many radios and observers heard, and keeps each message once, with every reception of it. Read your messages in the MECON Reader, a web app, from any device.

See the network and its limits

Which radios carry the traffic, where paths break and where coverage is thin. MECON shows what its receivers actually heard, never more.

Look after radios from afar

Health, battery and firmware for every radio in one list. Configure and update supported radios, including those on a hilltop that only radio can reach.

Explore together, your way

Use a hosted service, run it on a small computer at home, or join several sites. The ideas, documents and radio firmware are shared openly for others to reuse.

See the network come alive

A map of the radios MECON's observers hear, and the messages moving between them, is planned for this page. It is not here yet, and nothing on this page is live.

  • Observed nodes are radios that some observer has heard. A radio no observer hears will not appear, so the map will never be the whole mesh.
  • Recent packets will show activity, not message content. Private messages stay encrypted.
  • Coverage is partial. Observers sit where volunteers put them, so some areas look quiet only because nobody is listening there.
  • A location can be unknown. A radio will be placed only if it shares its own position. Where it was heard from is not where it is.

Why MECON exists

MECON is an independent, non-commercial experiment: made to try ideas, learn, share what works and inspire others to build on it. It has no revenue objective. mecon.cloud may one day ask for voluntary help with hosting costs; that is not a subscription.

How it fits together

MECON is an optional layer around MeshCore. It does not replace MeshCore's packet formats, radio routing, node identities or normal Companion apps. Everything inside the dashed boundary is optional; the MeshCore radios keep talking to each other without it.

Ways to run it

From someone else's server to infrastructure of your own. In every case your radios keep working as ordinary MeshCore radios if MECON goes away. Deployment choices

Hosted

A hosted service runs the Backend, Reader and broker. You bring your Companion, and optionally a gateway radio at home.

A home site

One small computer on your network runs the Backend, Reader and a local broker. Nothing has to be reachable from the Internet.

Several sites

Each site runs its own Backend, and they share records as peers, with brokers on the Internet for reach and resilience.

The firmware alone

Run mecon-firmware with a manager of your own, using its open device contracts.

Getting involved

Read the documentation, review the device contracts in mecon-firmware, describe a use case, or express interest in testing. Documentation feedback and contract review are welcome now.
